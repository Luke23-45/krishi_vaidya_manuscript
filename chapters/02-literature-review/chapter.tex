\ChapterSubsection{Crop Disease Diagnosis as a Decision-Support Problem}

The Crop disease diagnosis in the field includes more than just the image classification. Farmers need immediate intervention strategies like specific chemicals that can be sprayed, dosage and how fast it should be sprayed, which the computer vision model cannot provide at their current state. Despite a lot of higher accuracy in the identification of plant pathogens in computer vision models, researchers rarely try to map these biological labels to practical agriculture practices. This gives rise to an application gap between the prediction done by algorithms and the decision support required from them. Nepal's agriculture accounts for a quarter of the national GDP, and most rural households depend on it\parencite{moaldAgricultureStats2080,nsoAgricultureCensus2021}.
Smallholder farmers of Nepal operate under strict budget constraints. And if the farmer selects the wrong pesticide or delays a response to a fungal disease can lead to loss of income \parencite{pandeyCropLossNepal2022}. 

Since the farmer's losses can be minimised using digital software, the software would be deployed depending on local infrastructure. From ICT analysis and studies by FAO, even as the number of farmers with smartphones grows, network connectivity is segmented in rural areas. Another issue would be a complicated user interface and software that only consists of English text, which make the farmers give up using agricultural applications eventually (Sharma, Singh, Rana and Kumar, 2017). Though having higher model accuracy in the test environment does not solve anything if deployment is limited due to local infrastructure. It would have practical implications if the diagnosis could be performed offline and local instructions written in Nepali, as well treatments, could be provided directly as steps instead of only the name of the disease \parencite{silvestriSmallholderTechAdoption2021}.

\ChapterSection{Literature Review}

\ChapterSubsection{Deep Learning for Plant Disease Detection}

\subsubsection{Dataset evolution and the lab-to-field gap}

Deep learning for plant pathology has increased with the PlantVillage study. Convolutional neural networks achieved over 99 percent accuracy in classifying 26 diseases with across 14 crop species \parencite{mohanty2016plantvillage}. Even though these results looked like a breakthrough. The images taken by the user can be varied based on the user device, environment, lighting and other factors, which would be different from the dataset that was used for the training; therefore, the model would likly the collapsed on these outlier data \parencite{singhPlantDoc2020,thakurPlantDiseaseFieldGap2022}.

The researchers have collected ``in-the-wild'' disease images from different sources online, and are trying to prove that standard transfer-learning models struggle outside the experimental environment \parencite{singhPlantDoc2020}.  Best-in-class machine learning models reach only 60 to 75 percent. This huge performance drop, often exceeding 25 percentage points, now opens up agricultural machine learning research \parencite{thakurPlantDiseaseFieldGap2022,li2021plantDiseaseReviewCNN}.

The machine learning models evolved from AlexNet and VGGNet through ResNet \parencite{heResNet2016}, to current models such as EfficientNet architectures that rely heavily on ImageNet pre-training \parencite{saleem2019plantDiseaseML,liu2021plantDiseaseReview}. Despite this, choosing the best-performing model means very little, and the real performance depends on the quality of the dataset and how the classes are balanced, including the specific augmentation tricks used during training. And testing these models on the original PlantVillage dataset inflates accuracy therefore, the true evaluations require messy, field-collected data \parencite{thakurPlantDiseaseFieldGap2022}.


\subsubsection{Existing mobile disease-detection systems}

Different mobile applications have tried to deliver deep-learning-based plant disease diagnosis
to farmers. Plantix, developed by PEAT, uses a convolutional neural network to classify disease,
nutrient deficiency, and pest damage from photographs, with treatment advice delivered in
multiple languages \parencite{petrellaPlantix2023}. PlantVillage Nuru, developed by Penn State
University in partnership with IITA and the FAO, provides on-device inference using TensorFlow
Lite and was specifically designed for offline use a critical feature for farmers in remote

\ChapterSubsection{Object Detection in Agricultural Vision}

\subsubsection{The YOLO detector family}

The image that is taken for disease detection from the farm often contains different variables like background, crop, shadows, lighting and others, which act as noise. Therefore, object detection is essential to locate the specific region of the plant where the disease is more likely, such as leaves, panicles, or fruits; the object detector prevents overwhelming the diagnostic models with irrelevant background data.

In the agricultural context, you need a model that can provide accurate results at a faster speed. For this requirement, the YOLO models fit perfectly \parencite{jiangYOLOSurvey2022}. The field deployment of the YOLO model would often have mAP@50 between 0.7 and 0.92 \parencite{tian2020AppleFruitYOLO,zhengSmallObjDetAgri2023}. For the Krishi Vaidya YOLOv8 nano was used (\texttt{yolov8n}) \parencite{ultralyticsYolov8}. It has 3.2 million parameters and is able to run on the end devices without cloud support. 

\subsubsection{Detection evaluation methodology}

The model evaluation results require more than one metric to convey its performance \parencite{padillaMetricsSurvey2021}. Therefore, in the Krishi Vaidya project, we have followed different metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) across standard Intersection-over-Union (IoU) thresholds \parencite{everinghamPASCALVOC2010,linCOCO2014}. And additional metrics such as precision, recall, and the F1-score.

\subsubsection{Small-object and dense-detection challenges}

Some crops, such as rice grains clustered or clumped, or rice panicles, which would be so close to each other and densely collected targets, still remain a challenge in agricultural computer vision \parencite{zhengSmallObjDetAgri2023}. Also, while preparing the data for rice grains or crops that have dense regions, it would be difficult to label and draw a box for each of them, which also adds noise to the dataset itself. 

\subsubsection{Quantization and deployment}

Running a neural network requires a dedicated amount of compute. And to run on lower-end devices like smartphones, which do not possess lots of compute power, it would be challenging to run the neural network on them. Therefore, a quantisation approach would allow a version of the neural network to run on the low end since it would reduce the size. A full-precision floating-point (FP32) would be converted into smaller formats such as FP16 or INT8. It would reduce the size, which would increase the inference speed but at the cost of performance \parencite{garg2022quantizationSurvey}. 
For the quantisation of the trained yolo model, we used Ultralytics \parencite{ultralyticsExport}.

After we have deployed the quantised version of the yolo model, we have tested it thoroughly, and from the experimental results we discovered that quantisation would reduce the accuracy. \parencite{paleyes2022MLSystemsChallenges}.

\ChapterSubsection{Vision-Language Models for Structured Diagnosis}

\subsubsection{Structured reasoning}

Before the VLM, YOLO and other vision models were used for disease detection, and they could only work as pure classification models, as they output the disease label. With the VLM, since they can reason like an LLM and provide detailed context about the given image, not only the disease label. They can look at the crop image and provide the description by breaking down the image in detail. LLaVA introduced instruction tuning because of which the VLM can do beyond disease label predictions \parencite{liuLLaVA2023}. With this, now the VLM can be treated as the  Visual Question Answering (VQA) \parencite{zhangVLMSurvey2024}. 
The Agri-LLaVA learned mappings between pests and diseases based on multi-phase instruction tuning \parencite{wangAgriLLaVA2024}. In the LLMI-CDP, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with domain-specific formatting yields higher diagnostic accuracy \parencite{yangLLMCropDisease2024}. 

\subsubsection{Parameter-efficient fine-tuning}

Since VLMs work with images, they require lots of compute; therefore, different techniques can be adapted for fine-tuning, and one is LoRA. In this approach to fine-tuning, you could freeze the base model weights and keep them as they are. And insert the trainable adapter matrices into the specific attention and feed-forward layers \parencite{hu2021lora}. 

The size of the VLM is often larger; therefore, fitting them into memory for fine-tuning could be challenging. Therefore, to resolve these issues, an approach called QLoRA could be used. As it would quantise the frozen base weights of the VLM to 4-bit precision  \parencite{dettmers2023qlora}.
Due to compute constraints, in the Krishi Vaidya project we use both QLoRA and LoRA processes on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU.
Even though fine-tuning would be possible with this approach, inference of the VLM still demands lots of compute in the agricultural context, which is still challenging \parencite{zhuangTransferLearningSurvey2021}.

\ChapterSubsection{Offline-First Mobile Architecture}

Agricultural mobile apps in developing regions face constant failures that are related to infrastructure, since it has lots of components like the YOLO model, VLM, mobile app, and backend. Also, the mobile app should work or run even when it is not connected to the internet. \textcite{kleppmannLocalFirst2019} outlines the ``local-first'' software paradigm to solve this dependency. 


\ChapterSubsection{Backend Architecture and ML System Design}

 The real-world applications often require heavy transformation to turn raw model outputs into structured data. A server must authenticate incoming requests before directing the model service, parsing outputs, and updating the database. 

\textcite{sculleyMLTechDebt2015} presented the ``hidden technical debt'' of ML systems.  The major engineering effort goes into the surrounding infrastructure to manage data collection, build active serving setups, and handle complex configurations. The deployment of the end-to-end system complexity could extend deep into data pipelines and organisational limits \parencite{paleyes2022MLSystemsChallenges}.

\ChapterSubsection{Research Gap and Positioning}

The Krishi Vaidya project focuses on working on the following gaps, which are given below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Integration gap:} \\
    Most of the available crop health detection systems are not designed around farmers since most of them are in the form of machine learning models, and crop management mobile app like Plantix and PlantVillage Nuru only provides crop management features. \parencite{petrellaPlantix2023,mrishoPlantVillageNuru2020,mohanty2016plantvillage} Therefore, there is no system that was built end-to-end for crop health detection via a user interface. 
    
    \item \textbf{Structured-output evaluation gap:} \\
    There are tools based on LLMs and vlm which can provide accuracy. \parencite{wangAgriLLaVA2024,yangLLMCropDisease2024}. But for the crop health detection system, accuracy will not be enough; it needs to be able to provide detailed results as well, in the form of JSON and other structured data structures. 
    
    \item \textbf{Contextual deployment gap:} \\
    Most of the projects around the crop health detection system were built on the assumption without having prior knowledge of farmers' requirements, such as an easy user interface, language support, and others. Most of the published benchmarks about crop health detection do not include the real-world setbacks. \parencite{thakurPlantDiseaseFieldGap2022}
\end{itemize}

With the Krishi Vaidya project, we are designing a system for crop health detection with different components like vlm, yolo models, a mobile app, and backends.
